\title{CiM-ResLDPC: Residual Error Calibrated Robust Retrieval-Augmented Generation on NOR Flash Compute-in-Memory Arrays}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Edge retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) increasingly relies on compute-in-memory (CiM) to keep private corpora on-device.
Dense CiM substrates (including NOR Flash targets) offer capacity for local knowledge bases, but multi-cycle readout noise, quantization to constrained alphabets, and analog residuals corrupt stored embeddings and collapse ranking quality.
Prior CiM-RAG systems mainly accelerate similarity with in-array MAC and treat noise as a shallow mask; they rarely protect ternary embeddings with non-binary LDPC or feed soft residual evidence into dense scoring.

We present \textbf{CiM-ResLDPC}, a hardware--IR co-design on the GRC-PIM multi-cycle PIM channel.
Document embeddings are packed into GF(3) symbols, LDPC-protected for CiM storage, and recovered via B$'$ soft LLRs and/or belief propagation (optionally with gated GEMV residual $\Delta$LLR).
Queries remain digital (\emph{asymmetric} RAG).
On a MSMARCO passage subset (1000 queries / 8503 passages, BGE-small teacher, typical NOR-mapped stress), unprotected CiM noise drops nDCG@10 to 0.283; LDPC BP recovers to 0.496 ($+$0.214), and hybrid BM25$+$BP reaches 0.549 above BM25 0.520.
Multi-seed BP gains are $0.182\pm0.031$ (128-D) and $0.180\pm0.029$ (256-D).
Gains are operating-point and seed dependent; we report failures rather than hide them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% Full prose: docs/paper/INTRODUCTION.md
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is becoming the default way to ground LLMs in private knowledge.
On edge devices, local-first constraints forbid uploading raw documents, while interactive assistants still demand low retrieval latency.
Compute-in-memory (CiM) is attractive: dense embeddings can be stored and scored inside memory arrays.
NOR Flash CiM offers higher density than SRAM CiM at acceptable cost.

The difficulty is that CiM is not noiseless RAM.
When document embeddings live in analog arrays, hardware noise directly corrupts the vector geometry that dense retrieval depends on.
On MSMARCO with a BGE teacher projected to 128-D, unprotected CiM noise drops nDCG@10 from 0.641 to 0.283 at 1000 queries under a typical NOR-mapped stress point.

Existing CiM-RAG systems primarily treat the array as a MAC accelerator.
Classical storage coding repairs Flash bitstreams but is rarely connected to embedding reconstruction or ranking.
We propose \textbf{CiM-ResLDPC}, which packs embeddings into GF(3), protects them with non-binary LDPC, and recovers via B$'$/BP soft paths on the GRC-PIM channel, with optional hybrid BM25 fusion.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1)~TONEL-Res / CiM-aware representation path without clean-embedding oracles;
(2)~end-to-end LDPC-protected ternary retrieval under NOR-mapped noise;
(3)~edge system modes (decode gating, hybrid fusion) and generative RAG probes.

\section{Related Work}
% Full prose: docs/paper/RELATED_WORK.md
We position against (i)~dense retrieval and RAG, (ii)~CiM / PIM accelerators for similarity, and (iii)~non-binary LDPC and Flash ECC.
CiM-ResLDPC is complementary to GRC-PIM: GRC-PIM is the reliability layer; CiM-ResLDPC is the IR layer that consumes soft residual signals for ranking.

\section{Method}
% Full prose: docs/paper/METHOD.md
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Teacher encode (BGE/MiniLM) $\rightarrow$ project to edge width \texttt{cim\_dim}.
  \item Base-3 GF(3) pack (2 symbols/dim).
  \item LDPC encode with shipped GF(3) NB-LDPC.
  \item Multi-cycle CiM channel (GRC-PIM noise; NOR presets map stress).
  \item Recover: B$'$ and/or BP; optional GRC GEMV gated $\Delta$LLR.
  \item Asymmetric cosine retrieval (digital query vs recovered docs).
  \item Optional hybrid: BM25 $+$ dense $+$ residual-confidence fusion.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
Protocol: asymmetric RAG; BGE-small-en-v1.5 $\rightarrow$ random-project to \texttt{cim\_dim} $\rightarrow$ base-3 GF(3) $\rightarrow$ multi-cycle NOR-mapped channel $\rightarrow$ B$'$/BP.
Primary corpus: MSMARCO HF validation subset, \textbf{1000 queries / 8503 passages}.

BP recovers $+0.214$ nDCG@10 vs unprotected noise.
Multi-seed BP gain (seeds $\{0,1,2,42\}$): $0.182\pm0.031$ at 128-D; $0.180\pm0.029$ at 256-D.

\subsection{Scale ladder}
At the same protocol (dim=128, seed=42): 200q $+0.086$; 500q $+0.116$; 1000q $+0.214$.
Hybrid exceeds BM25 at each scale.

\subsection{Operating-point honesty}
Under aged/stress NOR maps, ranking can still degrade even when SER improves.
We report the operating point and historical pathological seeds (e.g., 500q dim=128 seed=0).

\section{Conclusion}
% Full prose: docs/paper/CONCLUSION.md
CiM-ResLDPC shows that residual soft information from CiM channels can be turned into measurable retrieval robustness for private on-device RAG, when claims are tied to explicit noise operating points.
Limitations include subset (not full Dev) scale, engineering NOR presets (not TCAD), random projection, and frozen-teacher IR tables.
