\title{CiM-ResLDPC: Residual Error Calibrated Robust Retrieval-Augmented Generation on NOR Flash Compute-in-Memory Arrays}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{%

\begin{abstract}
Edge retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) increasingly relies on compute-in-memory (CiM) to keep private corpora on-device.
Dense CiM substrates (including NOR Flash targets) offer capacity for local knowledge bases, but multi-cycle readout noise, quantization to constrained alphabets, and analog residuals corrupt stored embeddings and collapse ranking quality.
Prior CiM-RAG systems mainly accelerate similarity with in-array MAC and treat noise as a shallow mask; they rarely protect ternary embeddings with non-binary LDPC or feed soft residual evidence into dense scoring.

We present \textbf{CiM-ResLDPC}, a hardware--IR co-design on the GRC-PIM multi-cycle PIM channel~\cite{grcpim2025}.
Document embeddings are packed into GF(3) symbols, LDPC-protected for CiM storage, and recovered via B$'$ soft LLRs and/or belief propagation (optionally with gated GEMV residual $\Delta$LLR).
Queries remain digital (\emph{asymmetric} RAG).
On MSMARCO passage subsets with a BGE-small teacher~\cite{xiao2023bge} under typical NOR-mapped stress, unprotected CiM noise causes large nDCG@10 drops; LDPC BP recovers ranking (e.g., $+0.221$ at 2000 queries / 16894 passages; $+0.214$ at 1000q), and hybrid BM25$+$BP exceeds BM25 alone.
Multi-seed BP gains remain positive at 128-D and 256-D.
Gains are operating-point and seed dependent; we report failures rather than hide them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)~\cite{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm} is becoming the default way to ground large language models in private or long-tail knowledge.
On edge devices, \emph{local-first} constraints forbid uploading raw documents to the cloud, while interactive assistants still demand low retrieval latency.
Compute-in-memory (CiM) is an attractive substrate~\cite{sebastian2017pcm,shafiee2016isaac}: dense embeddings can be stored and scored inside memory arrays, avoiding costly off-chip data movement.
NOR Flash CiM is especially appealing for on-device knowledge bases because it offers higher density than SRAM CiM at acceptable cost.

The difficulty is that CiM is not a noiseless RAM.
Floating-gate / analog arrays exhibit read disturb, conductance drift, quantization, and analog MAC residuals.
When document embeddings live in such arrays, hardware noise is not a minor implementation detail---it \emph{directly corrupts the vector geometry that dense retrieval depends on}.
In our measurements on MSMARCO~\cite{nguyen2016msmarco} with a BGE teacher projected to a 128-D edge width, unprotected CiM noise drops nDCG@10 from 0.641 to 0.283 at 1000 queries under a typical NOR-mapped stress point.

Existing CiM-RAG systems primarily treat the array as a MAC accelerator for similarity.
Noise handling, when present, is often a shallow mask or averaging step.
In parallel, classical storage coding (including non-binary LDPC~\cite{davey1998nbldpc}) repairs raw Flash bitstreams but is rarely connected to embedding reconstruction or ranking.
The result is a three-way gap: (i)~hardware noise $\leftrightarrow$ representation; (ii)~alphabet / bit-width mapping from float teachers to GF($q$) storage; (iii)~privacy $\leftrightarrow$ latency under edge budgets.

We propose \textbf{CiM-ResLDPC}, a hardware--IR co-design that reuses the GRC-PIM gated residual stack~\cite{grcpim2025} as a retrieval reliability layer.
Document embeddings are packed into GF(3) symbols, protected by non-binary LDPC, exposed to a multi-cycle CiM channel (with NOR operating-point presets), and recovered via B$'$ soft LLRs and/or belief propagation---optionally with gated GEMV residual $\Delta$LLR.
Queries remain on the digital side (\textbf{asymmetric RAG}), matching realistic edge pipelines where only the corpus is CiM-resident.
A hybrid BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25} $+$ residual-calibrated dense scorer provides a practical system mode.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{TONEL-Res / CiM-aware representation path:} residual soft-information regularization toward \emph{channel-calibrated} reconstructions (no clean-embedding oracle), with GF(3)-compatible quantization.
  \item \textbf{End-to-end CiM-ResLDPC retrieval:} LDPC-protected ternary embeddings $+$ B$'$/BP recovery $+$ optional GRC GEMV, evaluated under dense-only scientific metrics.
  \item \textbf{Edge system optimizations:} decode gating / hybrid sparse--dense fusion, local-only privacy contract, and generative RAG probes (Flan-T5~\cite{raffel2020t5}) with measurable hallucination.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Empirical highlight.}
On MSMARCO (2000 queries / 16894 passages, BGE-small, \texttt{nor\_typical}, \texttt{cim\_dim}=128, seed=42), LDPC BP recovers $+0.221$ nDCG@10 versus unprotected CiM noise (0.254$\rightarrow$0.475); hybrid BM25$+$BP reaches 0.557 (above BM25 0.531).
Multi-seed BP gain is $0.145\pm0.050$.
At 1000q the same protocol yields $+0.214$ with multi-seed $0.182\pm0.031$ (128-D).
Gains remain operating-point and seed dependent---we report failures rather than hide them.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{LLM-oriented RAG and retrieval noise}
Modern RAG pipelines retrieve passages to condition LLM generation~\cite{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm}.
Most IR work models \emph{textual} noise or retrieval--generation mismatch, e.g.\ aligning noisy queries back to clean roots~\cite{campos2023capot}.
Edge local RAG adds a different noise source: \textbf{analog CiM storage and MAC}.
Text denoisers, query-alignment, and LLM-side filters do not correct corrupted \emph{document-embedding} coordinates stored in the array.

\subsection{Dense retrieval and edge constraints}
Dense encoders (DPR~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr}, Contriever~\cite{izacard2022contriever}, BGE~\cite{xiao2023bge}, Sentence-BERT~\cite{reimers2019sentencebert}) dominate MSMARCO-style ranking.
Edge deployment forces compression.
We adopt a transparent protocol---frozen teacher $\rightarrow$ fixed projection to \texttt{cim\_dim} $\rightarrow$ GF(3) packing---so measured gains can be attributed to the CiM protection/recovery path.

\subsection{CiM-accelerated retrieval}
CiM / PIM accelerators reduce data movement for similarity search~\cite{sebastian2017pcm,shafiee2016isaac}.
The closest prior work targets CiM-backed RAG directly: RoCR~\cite{qin2024rocr} makes document embeddings noise-resilient through contrastive, noise-aware \emph{training}, and CiMRAG~\cite{chiu2026cimrag} learns task-oriented noise-resilient embeddings (TONEL) for multi-domain edge RAG.
Both harden the \emph{representation}; neither protects stored ternary embeddings with an explicit error-correcting \emph{channel code} nor feeds multi-cycle soft residual evidence into recovery.
CiM-ResLDPC is complementary---it adds a GF(3) LDPC residual-recovery layer beneath the same asymmetric-RAG setting (our TONEL-Res regularizer is a residual-aware variant of the TONEL line).
SRAM CiM is quieter but capacity-limited; NOR Flash CiM is denser but noise-rich, and \textbf{CiM-ResLDPC targets the latter regime.}

\subsection{LDPC and soft residual calibration}
Non-binary LDPC and soft-decision decoding are mature in Flash controllers~\cite{gallager1962ldpc,davey1998nbldpc}.
GRC-PIM adds a gated residual GEMV calibrator on multi-cycle soft LLRs~\cite{grcpim2025}.
Prior art stops at bit/symbol reliability.
We ask whether the same soft residual evidence can \emph{reconstruct embedding geometry} and improve nDCG under CiM noise.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Problem}
Edge RAG stores document embeddings in CiM.
Multi-cycle analog readout (drift, disturb, quantization) corrupts vectors and collapses nDCG.
Prior CiM-RAG accelerates MAC but does not protect ternary embeddings with NB-LDPC or feed residual soft evidence into retrieval.

\subsection{CiM-ResLDPC pipeline}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Teacher encode (BGE/MiniLM) $\rightarrow$ project to edge width \texttt{cim\_dim}.
  \item Base-3 GF(3) pack (2 symbols/dim).
  \item LDPC encode with shipped GF(3) NB-LDPC (systematic $G$).
  \item Multi-cycle CiM channel (GRC-PIM noise; NOR presets map stress $\rightarrow$ $(p_{\mathrm{indep}},p_{\mathrm{drift}},T)$).
  \item Recover: B$'$ soft-LLR product and/or BP; optional GRC GEMV gated $\Delta$LLR before BP.
  \item Retrieve: asymmetric cosine (clean digital query vs.\ recovered doc vectors).
  \item Optional hybrid: BM25 cutoff $+$ dense $+$ residual-confidence fusion.
  \item Generate: extractive or Flan-T5 conditioned on top-$k$ passages.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Formalization}
\paragraph{GF(3) base-3 packing.} Each teacher coordinate $e\in[-1,1]$ maps to $s$ ternary digits with $L=3^{s}$ levels ($s{=}2\Rightarrow L{=}9$):
\begin{equation}
r=\Big\lfloor\tfrac{e+1}{2}(L-1)\Big\rceil,\quad r=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1} c_j\,3^{j},\; c_j\in\mathrm{GF}(3),
\end{equation}
dequantized as $\hat e=\tfrac{2r}{L-1}-1$. Multi-symbol packing preserves the cosine geometry that single-centroid ternary collapses.

\paragraph{Channel and drift.} Packed symbols are stored and read over $T$ cycles through the GRC-PIM noise channel~\cite{grcpim2025}; a global drift score $d_{\mathrm{global}}\in[0,1]$ measures cross-cycle bit disagreement.

\paragraph{Recovery.} The training-free B$'$ product accumulates per-cycle soft LLRs $\ell_i^{B'}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{i,t}$ with hard readout $\hat c_i=\arg\max_k \ell_{i,k}^{B'}$; GF(3) BP refines $\ell^{B'}$ into a posterior over the systematic prefix, whose information symbols are dequantized to $\hat e$.

\paragraph{Dual gate (latency prune).} The optional GEMV residual $\Delta$LLR is applied only when
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{USE\_GEMV}=\mathbb{1}[d_{\mathrm{global}}\ge\tau_d]\wedge\mathbb{1}[\bar g\ge\tau_g],
\end{equation}
and skipped when $d_{\mathrm{global}}<\tau_{\mathrm{skip}}$; otherwise recovery equals B$'$/BP by construction (no extra risk).

\paragraph{Hybrid fusion.} Final ranking fuses min-max--normalized ($\tilde n$) channels
\begin{equation}
s = \alpha\,\tilde n(\text{BM25}) + \beta\,\tilde n(\text{dense}) + \gamma\,\tilde n(\text{res\_conf}),
\end{equation}
with defaults $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(0.3,0.5,0.2)$.

\subsection{TONEL-Res}
Train/adapt embeddings with residual alignment to \emph{channel-calibrated} reconstructions $\hat e_{\mathrm{cal}}$ (B$'$/BP, never a clean-embedding oracle) plus a soft ternary quantization penalty:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ret}} + w\big(1-\cos(e,\hat e_{\mathrm{cal}})\big) + \lambda_q\,\overline{\min_{l\in\{-1,0,1\}}\lvert e-l\rvert},
\end{equation}
with a dynamic residual weight driven by drift and gate confidence,
\begin{equation}
w=\mathrm{clip}\big(0.25\min(d_{\mathrm{global}}/\tau_d,2)+0.5\,\bar g,\;0,1\big).
\end{equation}
In large-scale tables we evaluate frozen teachers $+$ projection for IR fairness; TONEL remains the training-time regularizer for CiM-aware fine-tuning.

\subsection{What we do not claim}
NOR presets are engineering maps onto the GRC-PIM channel, not full floating-gate TCAD.
MSMARCO stratified subsets are not the full leaderboard protocol.
Flan-T5-small is a probe, not a production edge LLM.
BP SER reductions do not always imply nDCG gains at every noise corner.
We also do not run RoCR/CiMRAG head-to-head: they target personalization profile benchmarks with learned CiM-robust embeddings, whereas our channel-coding layer is evaluated on MSMARCO with frozen teachers, so the approaches are complementary rather than directly comparable.

\subsection{System cost (analytical)}
The on-chip residual-calibrator overhead is inherited from the GRC-PIM 28\,nm analytical estimates~\cite{grcpim2025}: $<\!0.05$\,mm$^2$ area, ${\sim}41\,\mu$W duty-cycled power, and $<\!3\,\mu$s latency---i.e.\ $<\!6.3\%$ area and $<\!3\%$ power versus the NB-LDPC decoder baseline.
The dual gate and decode-prune ($d_{\mathrm{global}}<\tau_{\mathrm{skip}}$ skips the GEMV path) duty-cycle this cost, so the \emph{average} overhead is well below the peak.
These are analytical estimates carried over from the GRC-PIM stack; a cycle-accurate CiM retrieval-latency model on a measured NOR array is future work, and our Python wall-clock timings are only a software proxy.

\section{Conclusion}
CiM-ResLDPC shows that residual soft information from CiM channels can be turned into measurable retrieval robustness for private on-device RAG, when claims are tied to explicit noise operating points.
Limitations include subset (not full Dev) scale, engineering NOR presets (not TCAD), random projection, and frozen-teacher IR tables.
Future work: full MSMARCO Dev/DL, learned GF(3)/PQ maps, measured NOR array noise, and on-device LLMs.
